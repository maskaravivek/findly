# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People in Canada, initially Ontario, who need to identify, compare, or locate a product without already knowing the exact retail terminology. They may be at home planning a repair, shopping under a same-day deadline, comparing an exact model, or standing inside a store. Their primary task is to describe what they need and quickly choose a trustworthy way to get it online or nearby.

## Product Purpose

Findly turns a plain-language shopping need, exact identifier, photo, or barcode into comparable product offers. Success means the user can understand what to buy, see whether each result is exact or merely compatible, know which price and availability claims were actually verified, and continue safely to the retailer. The current production slice focuses on natural-language search and live Home Depot Canada search-page offers while keeping unavailable capabilities explicit.

## Brand Personality

Practical, trustworthy, and clear. Findly should feel like a focused shopping tool that reduces uncertainty and respects the user's time, with calm confidence rather than conversational theatrics.

## Anti-references

Do not resemble a generic chatbot, an ad-heavy shopping portal, or a speculative AI demo. Avoid dead controls, fake live-data claims, hidden provenance, dense setup forms in the primary search flow, and indoor-navigation promises unsupported by retailer data.

## Design Principles

1. Start with the shopping job: one obvious way to describe the need and begin.
2. Make trust visible: show live verification, freshness, source, and capability limits near the result they qualify.
3. Infer routine context: use location and saved preferences quietly, with a clear settings fallback.
4. Ask only consequential questions: clarification should materially change the product recommendation.
5. Degrade honestly: partial, unverified, or unavailable data must remain useful without being presented as live.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Follow native Android and Material 3 accessibility conventions, support system font scaling and screen readers, maintain readable contrast, provide at least 48 dp touch targets, never rely on colour alone for status, and avoid decorative motion. Location permission denial must not block non-local search, and manual location configuration must remain available.
